About This Work

This project focused on studying the fuel spray characteristics of air-assist type injectors intended for use in scramjet engines, aiming to support the development of a scramjet engine equipped with regenerative cooling technology. Two types of injectors were tested, one ejecting fuel at a 90° angle and the other at a 60° angle relative to the main flow. We used Mie-scattering techniques for visualization and a Phase Doppler Particle Analyzer (PDPA) to measure atomization characteristics. Experimental results showed that higher supply gas pressure and greater distances from the nozzle led to improved atomization performance. In addition, the 60° injector demonstrated better atomization characteristics compared to the 90° injector.

Methodology

To identify the optimal injector configuration for scramjet combustion, we compared two types of air‑assist injectors under various pressure conditions. Spray visualization was performed using Mie‑scattering, and droplet size and velocity were measured with a Phase Doppler Particle Analyzer (PDPA) to evaluate atomization performance and spray characteristics.

Experimental Setup

The experimental rig consisted of a pressurized liquid and gas supply system, a custom injector mounting assembly, and two optic measurement tools: a Mie-scattering visualization setup and a PDPA system.
The Mie-scattering system utilized a Xenon lamp and ICCD camera to capture instantaneous spray images, which were later processed using Otsu’s method to extract spray boundaries. The PDPA system provided quantitative measurements of droplet velocity and size distribution across multiple axial positions. Together, these tools enabled a high-resolution analysis of spray behavior under varying injection conditions.

Experimental Conditions

Tests were conducted using water and nitrogen as simulants for fuel and air under standard ambient conditions. Liquid pressure was varied between 400–600 kPa and gas pressure between 430–800 kPa to observe changes in spray behavior. Measurements were taken at multiple axial distances from the nozzle (30–140 mm) to capture droplet breakup and dispersion under different operating conditions.

Results

Spray Visualization

Spray images captured with the Mie‑scattering system revealed that higher gas pressure increased both spray angle and spray area. One injector type showed a broader spray distribution, while the other produced a denser, more focused spray pattern. These differences were most evident near the nozzle and provide insight into potential mixing efficiency in a scramjet combustor.

Droplet Velocity Distribution

PDPA measurements showed that droplet velocity decreased with axial distance from the nozzle but increased with higher gas supply pressure. Differences between the two injector types diminished farther downstream (130 mm from the nozzle).

Droplet Size Distribution

The Sauter Mean Diameter (SMD) decreased both with increasing gas pressure and with greater distance from the nozzle. One injector consistently produced smaller droplets (up to 29% reduction in SMD), resulting in a finer, denser spray that is advantageous for combustion stability.


The injectors show clear droplet size differences at a fixed downstream location, confirmed by both visuals and measurements.
